# Exact eight-class reduction

**Status: PROVED.** All identities below are polynomial identities in
`c,d,s`. The rational substitutions in `derive_classes.py` are cross-checks,
not proofs.

Put $z=s^2$ and scale every atom probability by $576$:

\[
P_j=576p_j(c,d),\qquad Q_j=576q_j(c,d,s),\qquad D_j=Q_j-P_j.
\]

The representatives and multiplicities are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
j&A_j&m_j\\ \hline
1&\varnothing&2\\
2&\{1\}&12\\
3&\{1,2\}&12\\
4&\{1,2,3\}&2\\
5&\{1,4\}&12\\
6&\{2,4\}&6\\
7&\{1,2,4\}&12\\
8&\{1,3,4\}&6
\end{array}
\]

The center polynomials are

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_1={}&9(1-c^2)(1-d^2)^2,\\
P_2={}&3(1-d^2)\{3+d^2-c^2(1+3d^2)\},\\
P_3={}&3(1+d^2)\{3-d^2+c^2(1-3d^2)\},\\
P_4={}&9(1+c^2)(1+d^2)^2,\\
P_5={}&(d^2-2cd+3)^2-\{c(3d^2+1)-2d\}^2,\\
P_6={}&(1-d^2)\{(3+3cd)^2-(c+d)^2\},\\
P_7={}&9c^2d^4-2c^2d^2+c^2+8cd^3+8cd+d^4-2d^2+9,\\
P_8={}&(1+d^2)(9c^2d^2+c^2-16cd+d^2+9).
\end{aligned}
\]

The endpoint corrections $D_j=Q_j-P_j$ are

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_1={}&-216z(1-c^2)(1+d^2-6z),\\
D_2={}&72z\{d^2-1-c^2(3d^2+1)+6z(3c^2-1)\},\\
D_3={}&72z\{3c^2d^2+c^2+d^2-1-6z(3c^2+1)\},\\
D_4={}&-216z(1+c^2)(1+d^2-6z),\\
D_5={}&24z\{9c^2d^2-3c^2-4cd-d^2+3+6z(1-9c^2)\},\\
D_6={}&24z\{9c^2d^2-3c^2+8cd-d^2+3+6z(1-9c^2)\},\\
D_7={}&24z\{-9c^2d^2+3c^2-4cd-d^2+3+6z(9c^2+1)\},\\
D_8={}&24z\{-9c^2d^2+3c^2+8cd-d^2+3+6z(9c^2+1)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus $p_j=P_j/576$ and $q_j=(P_j+D_j)/576$. These formulas show
immediately that the endpoint law is even in $s$, so $Q_+$ and $Q_-$ agree
event by event.

## Why these are all the classes

Write $X=\{1,2,3\}$ and $Y=\{4,5,6\}$. Among the nine cross-block entries
of $C$, six equal $\alpha=(c+d)/3$ and three equal
$\beta=(c-2d)/3$. The three $\beta$-pairs are
$\{1,5\},\{2,4\},\{3,6\}$.

The coordinate symmetry group is generated by

\[
\rho=(1\,2\,3)(4\,6\,5),\quad
\tau=(1\,2)(4\,5),\quad
\sigma=(1\,4)(2\,5)(3\,6).
\]

It has order $12$. The generator $\rho$ fixes both $K$ and $B$, while
$\tau,\sigma$ fix $K$ and send $B$ to $-B$. An exact-event determinant of a
Hermitian matrix is real, so complex conjugation shows that changing $B$ to
$-B$ leaves every endpoint probability unchanged.

Complementation is also a symmetry. With
$J=\mathrm{diag}(I_3,-I_3)$,

\[
J(I-Q_+)J=Q_-.
\]

The particle-hole identity gives
$p_A(Q_+)=p_{A^c}(Q_-)=p_{A^c}(Q_+)$.

The orbits under coordinate symmetry and complementation are:

1. the empty and full sets;
2. one-element and five-element sets;
3. two points in the same block, and their complements;
4. the two whole blocks $X,Y$;
5. an $\alpha$-cross-pair, and its complement;
6. a $\beta$-cross-pair, and its complement;
7. a $2+1$ triple whose singleton has one $\alpha$-edge and one
   $\beta$-edge to the pair;
8. a $2+1$ triple whose two cross-edges are both $\alpha$-edges.

Their sizes are $2,12,12,2,12,6,12,6$, which sum to $64$. These descriptions
are disjoint, so the eight orbits exhaust all subsets.

## Determinant derivation

For $A\subseteq[6]$, let $I_{A^c}$ be the diagonal projection onto its
complement. The exact-event probability is

\[
p_A(Q)=(-1)^{|A^c|}\det(Q-I_{A^c}).
\]

Substitution of one representative of each orbit gives the displayed
polynomials. No division or genericity assumption occurs: expansion by the
Leibniz formula proves the identities over
$\mathbb Q[c,d,s,i]/(i^2+1)$. The imaginary parts cancel and every result is
even in $s$.

For a transparent sample, the eigenvalues of $C$ are $c,d,-d$, and $S_0$
annihilates the $c$-eigenline while coupling the $d,-d$ plane. Hence

\[
p_\varnothing(K)=\det(I-K)
=\frac{(1-c^2)(1-d^2)^2}{64}.
\]

On the coupled plane the two endpoint factors are $(1-d)^2-12s^2$ and
$(1+d)^2-12s^2$. Therefore

\[
q_\varnothing
=\frac{(1-c^2)\{(1-d)^2-12s^2\}
                  \{(1+d)^2-12s^2\}}{64},
\]

and subtraction gives $D_1/576$. The other representatives follow from the
same signed determinant formula.

The script `derive_classes.py` constructs all 64 symbolic determinants,
groups them by exact polynomial equality, verifies both normalizations, proves
that the group orbits coincide with the equality classes, and independently
checks the formulas at three rational points with a separate Leibniz
determinant over `Fraction` pairs.

Two useful identities, obtained by direct collection of the displayed
polynomials, are

\[
\sum_{j=1}^8m_jP_j=576,
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^8m_jD_j=0.
\]
